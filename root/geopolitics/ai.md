The relase of GPT 6 "Astra" from OpenAI marked a significant paradigm shift in the sentiment around AI. The model was able to do quite impressive things, such as using 3D modelling and chip design software to do the task of an intermediate human artist or chip designer. Following this release, OpenAI using an even better internal model published an example[@oai_ns] that causes a breakdown of physics in the Navier-Stokes differential equation where the energy is finite but the velocity becomes infinite. It was obvious to most that Navier-Stokes is not a truly physical model. After all, it assumes that fluids are a continuum, when in reality, fluids are made up of molecules. So the physics described by NS had to break down somehow at unrealistic conditions. That particular counter-example may not be something that changes the trajectory of humanity, but in the pre-AI world, it would have been something a college lab would have worked for 10 years and at least two students would have their Ph.D theses around it. Now, a corporation that employs mostly computer programmers with only compute at dispoal is able to compete with mathematicians and engineers who spent their entire lives working with Navier-Stokes.

In my own free time, I have been using these models to reverse engineer old software and video games. I have myself been able to decompile the original Blood (1996) game and see its internals and create something similar in an existing game engine like Godot. Many others have too, AI is able to put in Call of Duty guns in Minecraft[@melty]. Someone has used 2026 state-of-the-art AI models to recreate the whole Adobe Creative suite of software[@photocraft]. The "obscurity of scale" that a lot of software relied on is long ago. There is nothing particularly special about Microsoft Office or Adobe Photoshop. Special as in something they have truly innovated. Their value was in the scale of their software and their ability to provide customer support. The latter still exists but the former has been decimated. Software is a solved problem. Any new software idea you may have can be turned into a product with AI assistance rather quickly. The marginal value of ideas and their implementation is going to do down to the cost of inference. 

# The End of Meritocracy

The last 200 years of industrialization placed an immense value over human technical skill and ingenuity. A website designer writing HTML, CSS and JS that did not receive more than two visits per day or a software engineer who wrote code or documentation that may not even have executed or be read in a production environment, was more paid than a caterer in a restaurant serving hundreds of customers daily. This was called the meritocracy, where technical merit was placed on a pedestal and hard labour was undervalued.

Why? Because hard human labour was replaceable. Anyone without an intellectual or physical disability can serve hundreds of customers a day in a restaurant, but only a select few could write a TCP webserver or a CUDA kernel. Generally, it required years of experience with a computer and a few years of formal education to understand both the underlying mathematics as well as implementation details to be able to write such code. 

With AI though, all these are now coming down at the cost of inference. A meat proxy in charge of guiding AI agents to do maintainance and implementation is far more replacable now. 

Software engineers will be the first to fall, but so will everyone else. System administrators, Chip designers, Computational chemists, even the high level management teams in financial and legal firms will have their numbers cut. The highest paid jobs will be hit the hardest. 

# The Rise of Megacorporations and SOEs

Megacorporations and State-Owned-Enterprises (SOEs) will have a massive amount of compute and resources at dispoal. Their subsidiaries will generate and implement ideas faster than any independent entity could. 

The moat will no longer be having talent or expertise. It will be having compute, land, resources and legal rights.
